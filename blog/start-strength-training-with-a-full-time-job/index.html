How to start strength training when you work full-time

A simple plan for busy professionals: how often to train, what to do in each session, and how to keep going when work gets busy.

By Sash Nejad, certified personal trainer · 3 min read

Short answer

Start with two or three full-body sessions a week, about 45 minutes each. Pick five or six basic movements, keep a simple log, and add a little weight or one more rep when your sets start to feel manageable. Canada’s movement guidelines recommend muscle-strengthening activity at least twice a week, so two good sessions already count.

How often should a beginner lift weights?

Two or three times a week is plenty. The American College of Sports Medicine’s 2009 position stand recommended 2–3 days a week for people new to strength training. Its 2026 update puts consistency first: training all your major muscle groups at least twice a week matters more than finding a perfect program.

If your job is demanding, two sessions you never miss will do more for you than four you keep cancelling.

What should a beginner workout include?

A full-body session that covers the main ways your body moves:

  • A squat: goblet squat or leg press
  • A hinge: Romanian deadlift with dumbbells
  • A push: dumbbell bench press or push-ups
  • A pull: seated cable row or lat pulldown
  • Core or carry: plank or farmer’s carry

Two or three sets of each, with a weight you can lift for 8 to 12 reps, is a good place to start. That is the range the ACSM suggests for beginners.

Learn each movement before you chase heavier weights. If you are not sure about your form, film a set on your phone or ask a coach to check it.

How do you fit training around a full-time job?

  • Book it like a meeting. Put your sessions in your calendar at a fixed time and treat them like any other appointment.
  • Use the gym you already have. A condo gym with dumbbells, a bench and a cable machine is enough for your first months.
  • Have a short version ready. On a heavy week, do one or two sets of each exercise instead of skipping the session.
  • Train when your energy is best. Some people prefer before work; others go at lunch or straight after work, before they get home.

How do you know if you are making progress?

Write down every exercise, weight and number of reps. When you can do one or two more reps than your target on every set, add a little weight next time. The ACSM suggests increases of about 2–10% at that point.

A log turns “I think I’m getting stronger” into something you can see, which makes it much easier to keep going.

When is a coach worth it?

A coach helps most at the start, while you are learning the movements and building the habit. If you would like someone to build the plan around your schedule and check your form, that is what my coaching is for. You can also estimate your training weights with the free one-rep max calculator.
