How much protein do you need to build strength?

How much protein to eat when you start strength training, how to work out your number, and how to spread it across the day.

By Sash Nejad, certified personal trainer · 2 min read

Short answer

If you do strength training, aim for about 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight a day, with up to about 2.2 g/kg as an upper target. For a 70 kg (154 lb) person, that is about 112–154 g a day. The general guidance for healthy adults who do not train is about 0.8 g/kg.

Why does protein matter when you start lifting?

Strength training and protein work together. Both trigger muscle protein synthesis, the process your body uses to repair and build muscle, and the International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) notes the effect is greater when you eat protein before or after training.

A 2018 review of 49 studies found that people who added protein while strength training gained more strength and muscle than those who did not. The benefit levelled off at a total intake of about 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day.

How do you work out your number?

Multiply your weight in kilograms by 1.6 for your main target, and by 2.2 for the upper end. If you know your weight in pounds, divide it by 2.2 first.

Body weightProtein a day (1.6–2.2 g/kg)
60 kg (132 lb)96–132 g
70 kg (154 lb)112–154 g
80 kg (176 lb)128–176 g
90 kg (198 lb)144–198 g

How should you spread protein across the day?

Spread it over three or four meals rather than eating most of it at dinner. The ISSN suggests about 0.25 g per kg of body weight, or 20–40 g, per meal, every 3–4 hours.

Where can you get protein?

Good everyday options include chicken, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, tofu, tempeh, lentils, beans and lean meat. Canada’s food guide suggests filling a quarter of your plate with protein foods and choosing plant-based protein more often.

Do you need protein powder?

No. The ISSN notes that whole foods can cover the protein needs of active people. Powder is a practical option on days when food alone is hard, not a requirement.

Is more protein always better?

Not for building muscle: in the 2018 review, gains levelled off around 1.6 g/kg. Children, pregnant or breastfeeding people and people with certain health conditions, such as kidney disease, have different needs, so talk to a doctor or registered dietitian if that applies to you.
