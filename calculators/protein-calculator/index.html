How this calculator works

If you don’t strength train, the calculator uses the general guidance for healthy adults: about 0.8 g of protein per kg of body weight a day.

If you do strength training, it uses 1.6 g per kg as the main target and 2.2 g per kg as an upper target. A 2018 review of 49 studies found that extra muscle gained from strength training levelled off at about 1.6 g/kg a day, and the study’s margin of error reached about 2.2 g/kg. The International Society of Sports Nutrition recommends 1.4–2.0 g/kg a day for most people who exercise.

Strength training: weight (kg) × 1.6 to weight (kg) × 2.2 No strength training: weight (kg) × 0.8 Pounds to kilograms: weight (lb) ÷ 2.2

Questions

How much protein do I need a day to build muscle?

For most people who strength train, about 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day, which is about 0.7 g per pound. Some people may benefit from up to about 2.2 g per kg.

How should I split protein across the day?

Spread it over three or four meals. The International Society of Sports Nutrition suggests about 0.25 g per kg of body weight, or 20–40 g, per meal, every 3–4 hours.

Can I get enough protein from food alone?

Yes. Whole foods can cover the protein needs of active people. Protein powder is a convenient option when food alone is hard, not a requirement.

Who should check with a professional first?

Children, pregnant or breastfeeding people and people with certain health conditions, such as kidney disease, have different protein needs. If that applies to you, talk to a doctor or registered dietitian.
