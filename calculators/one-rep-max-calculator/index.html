How this calculator works

It uses the Epley formula, one of the most widely used equations for estimating a one-rep max from a set of several reps:

One-rep max = weight lifted × (1 + reps ÷ 30)

If you did a single rep, the weight you lifted is your one-rep max. The calculator stops at 10 reps because estimates get less reliable as the number of reps goes up.

Equations can also be less accurate for some lifts than others: a 1997 study that compared seven of them found they all underestimated the deadlift. Treat the result as a starting point and adjust from how your sets feel.

Questions

What is a one-rep max?

The heaviest weight you can lift for one full rep of an exercise with good form. It is often written as 1RM.

Should beginners test their one-rep max?

Usually not. Testing a true max takes time and is riskier for people who are new to heavy lifting. An estimate from a set of 3 to 10 reps is safer and good enough to plan your training.

How do I use my one-rep max?

Use the percentages to choose training weights. For building strength, the American College of Sports Medicine’s 2026 guidelines suggest heavier loads of around 80% of your one-rep max, for 2–3 sets per exercise.
