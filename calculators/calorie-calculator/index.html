How this calculator works

It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation to estimate the calories your body uses at rest, then multiplies that number by an activity factor to estimate what you burn in a typical day.

Men: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5 Women: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161

The activity factors are 1.2 (sedentary), 1.375 (lightly active), 1.55 (moderately active), 1.725 (very active) and 1.9 (extremely active). “Lose slowly” is 15% below your maintenance number and “gain slowly” is 10% above it.

A 2005 systematic review found that Mifflin-St Jeor was more likely than the other common equations to estimate resting metabolic rate within 10% of the measured value. It is still an estimate, and some people will be further off.

Questions

What are maintenance calories?

The number of calories you burn in a typical day, including exercise. If you eat about that much, your weight tends to stay roughly the same.

How accurate is this calorie calculator?

It gives an estimate, not a measurement. Use it as a starting point, then watch your weight for two to three weeks and adjust if it moves in a direction you did not expect.

Why does the formula ask for my sex?

The equation was built from measurements of men and women and uses a different number for each. If neither option fits you, try both and use a number between the two results.

Which activity level should I choose?

Pick the one that matches a typical week, not your best week. Most people with a desk job who train two or three times a week fit “lightly active” or “moderately active”.
